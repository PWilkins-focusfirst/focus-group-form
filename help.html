Getting Started

Plain-English guide to running your books. No accounting background needed.

🚀 Do this first (about 15 minutes)

You only do these once. After that it's just a quick monthly routine.

  1. Set up check printing. Open Print Checks → fill in Account & Bank Setup (your business name/address, South State routing & account number, and the number of your next blank check). Click Save setup.
    Your bank info is typed once and saved in your browser. It's never sent anywhere.
  2. Do a test print. On the same page, click Print alignment guide and print it on plain paper. Hold it up to the light against one of your real Office Depot check sheets. If it lines up, you're set. If not, nudge the Offset X/Y numbers (under "Advanced") and try again.
  3. Set your starting balance. Open the Ledger → in Account Setup type today's South State balance as the Opening balance, set the As of date, and click Save.
  4. Bring in your history. Open Import Transactions and load a CSV from each account (see the import guide below). Start with South State and Amex — that covers most of the picture.
  5. Look at your first P&L. Open Profit & Loss, pick a date range, and see your revenue, expenses, and profit.

🔁 Your monthly routine (about 10 minutes)

Once a month, after your statements are ready:

  1. Download last month's CSV from each service (South State, Amex, Meta, Tremendous, Square). Each has an "Export" or "Download CSV" button — see each service's guide below.
  2. Import each file in Import Transactions. Pick the matching source, choose the file, click Preview, then Import. Re-importing the same file is safe — duplicates are skipped automatically.
  3. Tidy up categories in the Ledger. Anything marked uncategorized can be fixed by clicking the category and typing one.
  4. Run the P&L for the month and, if you like, click Export CSV or Print to save a copy for your records or your accountant.
  5. Back up (Ledger → Backup). Keep the file somewhere safe.
The golden rule to avoid double-counting: count each charge from one place. Importing South State + Amex covers almost everything. Use Meta / Tremendous / Square files only if you want extra detail — and if you do, mark the matching Amex/bank line as a Transfer so it isn't counted twice.

🖨️ How to print a check

Step by step
  1. Open Print Checks. (Do your one-time bank setup first.)
  2. Under Write a Check, type the payee (who you're paying), the amount, and a memo (what it's for). The written-out amount and check number fill in automatically.
  3. Load a blank check sheet into your printer.
  4. Click Record & Print check. This prints the check and logs it so the check number counts up next time. (Use Print (don't record) if you're just testing.)
  5. In the print box: choose Letter size, 100% / Actual size, margins None, and turn off "fit to page" and headers/footers.
About the numbers at the bottom of the check (the MICR line): banks prefer special magnetic ink. Regular printer ink usually works because banks scan the image, but check with South State before printing a lot of them.

📥 How to import transactions

"Importing" just means feeding a spreadsheet file (a CSV) from each account into your ledger, so you don't type anything by hand.

The basic steps (same for every source)
  1. Open Import Transactions.
  2. Pick the Source that matches your file (e.g. "South State").
  3. Click Choose file and select the CSV you downloaded.
  4. The app fills in the column mapping for you. Usually you can leave it as-is.
  5. Click Preview import. Review the list — green IN is money in, red OUT is money out, purple XFER is a transfer (ignored in profit).
  6. Click Import. Done — it's now in your ledger.
Where to download each CSV
South StateLog in → your checking account → look for Download / Export (choose CSV or "Spreadsheet"). This is your revenue and most expenses.
AmexLog in → Statements & Activity → Download → CSV/Excel. These are your card expenses.
Meta (Facebook)Ads Manager → Reports / the export button → export the campaign spend as CSV.
TremendousDashboard → Rewards / Orders → export CSV. These are participant gift cards.
SquareDashboard → Reports / Transactions → export CSV. (Imported as transfers, since the money is already counted as a bank deposit.)
Not sure a file will work? Just try it — nothing is saved until you click Import, and the preview shows exactly what will happen. If the columns look wrong, you can change them, or send the file's top row to your helper to build an exact match.

📊 How to read your Profit & Loss

Open Profit & Loss and pick a period at the top (This month, This year, etc.). You'll see:

RevenueMoney the business earned, grouped by category.
ExpensesMoney the business spent, grouped by category.
Net profitRevenue minus expenses. Green is a profit, red is a loss.

Transfers (moving your own money between accounts) are left out on purpose, with a note showing how many were excluded. Use Export CSV or Print to hand it to your accountant.

💾 Keeping your data safe (important)

Right now everything is saved only in the browser on this computer. It is not in the cloud yet. That means:

  • Use the same browser on the same computer each time.
  • About once a month, open the Ledger and click Backup. Keep that file somewhere safe (Google Drive, email it to yourself, etc.).
  • If you ever switch computers, use Restore to load your backup.
Clearing your browser history/data can erase this. A recent backup is your safety net. (A cloud version using Google Sheets is planned so this becomes automatic.)

📖 Plain-English glossary

TransactionOne line of money moving — a payment, a charge, a deposit.
CategoryWhat a transaction was for (Advertising, Travel, Venue…). Categories are what group your P&L.
TransferMoving your own money between your accounts (e.g., paying your Amex bill from the bank). It's not income or an expense, so it's excluded from profit.
Revenue / IncomeMoney coming in to the business.
ExpenseMoney going out for business costs.
CSVA spreadsheet file you download from a bank or service. The importer reads it so you don't type things in.
ClearedA checkmark meaning the money has actually gone through at the bank (useful when matching against a statement).
MICR lineThe stylized numbers along the bottom of a check that the bank's machines read (your routing and account number).
LedgerThe full list of every transaction in one place.
P&LProfit & Loss — the report showing revenue, expenses, and whether you made a profit.
